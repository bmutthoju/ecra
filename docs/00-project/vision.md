# ECRA — Project Vision

> Status: APPROVED
> Authority: NORMATIVE
> Generation: GEN1

## 1. Vision

ECRA exists to make claims, assertions, and other forms of stated information
more reliable by providing a systematic way to evaluate them against relevant
evidence, authoritative sources, and context.

The goal is not merely to determine whether a statement appears plausible.
The goal is to make the basis for an evaluation explicit, traceable, and
verifiable.

ECRA is intended to provide a trustworthy foundation for applications that
need to determine whether information is adequately supported by evidence and
whether the evidence has been represented accurately and in its proper
context.

---

## 2. The Problem

Information systems increasingly consume, generate, transform, and communicate
claims.

A claim can appear credible while still being problematic because:

- it has no supporting evidence;
- the cited source is not authoritative or appropriate for the claim;
- the source does not actually support the claim;
- the source has been misquoted;
- relevant context has been omitted;
- a statement has been interpreted beyond what its source establishes;
- evidence is presented without sufficient provenance;
- multiple pieces of evidence are combined in a way that creates an
  unsupported conclusion;
- an authentic source is presented in a way that materially misrepresents what
  it establishes;

These problems are particularly significant when information is generated,
summarized, transformed, or communicated by automated systems.

A reliable system therefore needs more than text generation or simple
source matching. It needs a disciplined mechanism for evaluating claims,
evidence, provenance, and context.

---

## 3. The Core Idea

ECRA treats claims and their associated evidence as explicit objects of
evaluation. Evidence may support, contradict, qualify, or fail to establish
a claim.

Conceptually:

    Claim
       |
       v
    Evidence
       |
       v
    Source / Provenance
       |
       v
    Context
       |
       v
    Evaluation
       |
       v
    Verifiable Result

The evaluation should make it possible to understand not only the resulting
assessment, but also the evidence and reasoning basis from which that
assessment was produced.

ECRA therefore emphasizes:

- evidence;
- source authenticity and authority;
- provenance;
- contextual integrity;
- explicit evaluation;
- traceability;
- reproducibility;
- deterministic and testable behavior.

---

## 4. What ECRA Seeks to Enable

ECRA is intended to provide a reusable foundation for systems that need to:

- evaluate claims against evidence;
- determine whether cited evidence actually supports a claim;
- identify unsupported or insufficiently supported claims;
- detect potentially misleading use of sources;
- preserve relevant context when evaluating evidence;
- maintain provenance between claims, evidence, and sources;
- produce structured evaluation results;
- make evaluation results auditable and reproducible.

The reference implementation should provide these capabilities through explicit,
well-defined contracts and behavior rather than through implicit or
application-specific conventions.

---

## 5. Trust Through Traceability

ECRA's fundamental trust model is based on traceability.

A meaningful evaluation should be traceable from:

    Evaluation Result
          |
          v
       Claim
          |
          v
      Evidence
          |
          v
    Source / Provenance
          |
          v
       Context

The system should make it possible to determine what was evaluated, what
evidence was considered, where that evidence originated, and how the
evaluation relates to the available evidence and context.

ECRA does not treat an assertion as reliable merely because it is confidently
stated, frequently repeated, generated by an automated system, or associated
with a source citation.

---

## 6. Reference Implementation Vision

Generation 1 will establish a production-quality reference implementation of
the ECRA architecture.

The reference implementation is intended to demonstrate that the ECRA
requirements and architecture can be implemented as a coherent, testable,
observable, maintainable system.

It should provide a concrete foundation that:

- faithfully implements the approved Generation 1 specification;
- demonstrates the intended architectural boundaries;
- provides explicit and testable contracts;
- produces reproducible behavior;
- provides sufficient observability for operational use;
- can be understood and extended by human developers and AI-assisted
  development tools.

The reference implementation is the canonical executable realization of the
Generation 1 specification.

---

## 7. Long-Term Direction

Generation 1 establishes the foundation.

Future generations may expand ECRA's capabilities, integrations, and ecosystem
based on experience gained from the reference implementation.

Such future work must not be assumed to be part of Generation 1.

The Generation 1 objective is to establish a sound, complete, production-quality
foundation before expanding the scope.
